## Underrated context (Crash Course skips this)

- **Seymour Papert and _Mindstorms_ (1980)** — visionary: computers as "objects to think with" for children. LOGO turtle graphics → Scratch → Code.org all descend from him.
- **The MOOC hype cycle (2012–2016)** crashed. Coursera, edX, Udacity were going to "replace universities." Completion rates were < 10%. Lesson: knowing isn't enough — motivation and community matter.
- **Bloom's 2-sigma problem** (1984): students with 1-on-1 tutoring perform 2 standard deviations better than classroom students. Replicating this at scale is the holy grail of edtech. LLMs (Khanmigo, 2023+) may be the first credible attempt.
- **Spaced repetition** (SuperMemo → Anki) is empirically the most efficient memorization method known. Underused outside language learning.
- **The replication crisis hit ed-tech too.** "Learning styles" (visual/auditory/kinesthetic) is not science. Many edtech claims don't survive scrutiny.
- **"Edutainment" is mostly entertainment.** Studies show kids learn less from "educational" games than from focused practice. Crash Course CS itself is a notable counter-example — actually dense.
- **Salman Khan + LLMs (Khanmigo, 2023)** — first mass-deployed AI tutor. Whether it works at scale is the open question of the decade.

## Articles

- **[Augmenting Long-Term Memory — Michael Nielsen](https://augmentingcognition.com/ltm.html)** — best modern essay on spaced repetition for serious topics.

## Books

- **Seymour Papert — _Mindstorms_** — visionary, decades ahead of its time.
- **Peter Brown, Henry Roediger, Mark McDaniel — _Make It Stick_** — cognitive science of learning.
- **Barbara Oakley — _A Mind for Numbers_** — practical learning techniques (basis for "Learning How to Learn" MOOC).
- **Cal Newport — _Deep Work_** — environment design for focused learning.

## Interactive / hands-on

- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/)** — spaced repetition. Use it.
- **[Learning How to Learn (Coursera, Barbara Oakley)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/learning-how-to-learn)** — free, the most popular MOOC ever.
