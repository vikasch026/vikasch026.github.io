## Underrated context (Crash Course skips this)

- **Turing machines aren't physical.** They're a mathematical abstraction — infinite tape, a head, a state table. They formalize "what is computation?" Modern computers are equivalent in power (Turing-complete) but bounded by finite memory.
- **The halting problem is profound.** Turing proved (1936) that no program can determine if an arbitrary program will halt. This is the first known _undecidable_ problem and has descendants everywhere (Rice's theorem: most non-trivial program properties are undecidable).
- **Church-Turing thesis.** Lambda calculus (Church) and Turing machines (Turing) are equivalent. _Any_ reasonable model of computation is equivalent. This is why all programming languages can compute the same things.
- **Turing was prosecuted for being gay.** The UK's "indecency" law led to his chemical castration in 1952, suicide in 1954. Posthumous pardon in 2013. Story of horrific waste.
- **The Turing test is dated.** Modern LLMs trivially pass naive Turing tests. The test was a thought experiment, not an operational benchmark.

## Books

- **Andrew Hodges — _Alan Turing: The Enigma_** — the definitive biography. Basis for _The Imitation Game_.
- **Charles Petzold — _The Annotated Turing_** — walks through Turing's 1936 paper line by line. _Highly recommended._
- **Douglas Hofstadter — _Gödel, Escher, Bach_** — for the bigger picture of computation, formal systems, and mind.
- **Michael Sipser — _Introduction to the Theory of Computation_** — textbook for going deeper.
