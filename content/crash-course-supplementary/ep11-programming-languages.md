## Underrated context (Crash Course skips this)

- **LISP (1958) is still alive.** Many "modern" features — garbage collection, dynamic typing, REPL, code-as-data — were in LISP from day one. McCarthy was 30 years ahead.
- **COBOL still runs the world.** ~70% of business transactions, most of US banking, the IRS, and Social Security run on COBOL. There's a chronic shortage of COBOL programmers and they earn $$$.
- **C (1972) eats them all.** Most languages on this list are dead or niche. C influenced everything that came after. Today's mainstream languages descend from C, LISP, or Smalltalk.
- **Language design is taste, not science.** Why is `=` assignment in C but equality in Pascal? Why are arrays 0-indexed (Dijkstra explained why) vs 1-indexed (FORTRAN)? Arbitrary choices that lock in forever.
- **The Sapir-Whorf hypothesis for code.** The language you think in shapes how you solve problems. Try LISP for a weekend; you'll see iteration differently.

## Books
