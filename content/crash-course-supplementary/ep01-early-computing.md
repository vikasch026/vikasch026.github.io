## Underrated context (Crash Course skips this)

Babbage's machines were designed to compute **mathematical tables** (logarithms, navigation tables) which were error-prone when done by hand. Errors in navigation tables literally killed sailors. _This is why anyone cared about mechanical computing in the 1800s._

## Articles

- [Computer History Museum — Timeline](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/) — canonical reference. Bookmark for every episode.
- [Stephen Wolfram on Ada Lovelace](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2015/12/untangling-the-tale-of-ada-lovelace/) — long, definitive, separates myth from fact.
- [The Babbage Engine (CHM)](https://www.computerhistory.org/babbage/) — interactive site.
- [Hollerith and the Birth of IBM (IEEE)](https://ethw.org/Herman_Hollerith) — punched cards, 1890 Census → IBM.
- [The History of Mathematical Tables (Trefethen, PDF)](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/tables.pdf) — short, fascinating context for _why_ this all mattered.

## Books

- Doron Swade — _The Difference Engine_ — most readable history of this era.
- Walter Isaacson — _The Innovators_ — Ch 1–2 cover Babbage/Lovelace; the rest covers roughly the full Crash Course playlist in 500 pages.
- James Essinger — _Ada's Algorithm_ — biographical, accessible.
