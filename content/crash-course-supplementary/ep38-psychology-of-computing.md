## Underrated context (Crash Course skips this)

- **Don Norman defined the field.** _The Design of Everyday Things_ (1988) is required reading for anyone who builds software. Affordances, signifiers, feedback, mappings — his vocabulary runs all of UX.
- **Fitts's Law** (1954): time to acquire a target ∝ distance / size. Why the Mac menu bar at the top of the screen is faster than Windows's: infinite height (you can't overshoot the edge).
- **Hick's Law**: time to decide ∝ log(number of choices). Why menus are nested, not flat.
- **The 10-second / 1-second / 100ms rules** (Jakob Nielsen): 100ms feels instant, 1s preserves flow, 10s loses attention. Web vitals (LCP, INP) codify these.
- **Cognitive load** matters more than feature count. The best UIs hide complexity progressively.
- **Accessibility is not optional.** ~15% of people have a disability. WCAG isn't bureaucratic — it's the difference between "users" and "all users."
- **The dark side**: persuasive design → manipulation → addiction. Slot-machine mechanics in apps (pull-to-refresh, infinite scroll, streaks). Tristan Harris and the Center for Humane Technology call it out.
